# Schubert Calculus, 1879 → today

Beat-synced motion-graphics films that race through the major results of
combinatorial Schubert calculus in chronological order. Every result shows its
year, its name, the paper or papers behind it, and an animated, mathematically
correct visual of the idea.

| cut | file | length | score |
|---|---|---|---|
| **75 seconds** (main) | `schubert-history-75s.mp4` · `film.html` | 75 s, 31 results | `score75.py` (original, 128 BPM) |
| 32 seconds (teaser) | `schubert-history-32s.mp4` · `film32.html` | 32 s, 28 results | `score.py` (original, 128 BPM) |

Both are 1920×1080 at 60 fps, rendered frame by frame with motion blur.

## The 75-second cut

In this cut the mathematical object owns the centre of the frame. The year,
headline and citations sit in a lower third, and a thin top bar carries a
1879 → today rail.

| time | beats | music | picture |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–7.5 s | 0–16 | intro: booms and toms | Cold open: *How many lines meet four given lines?* The first line slashes through the question; four lines land on the toms, two transversals ignite, **= 2**. |
| 7.5–22.5 s | 16–48 | verse | The classical era, one bar per result (1886 – 1959). |
| 22.5–30 s | 48–64 | build: snare roll, riser | 1973, 1977, then **THE COMBINATORIAL TURN** slams in word by word through a tunnel of boxes; one beat of silence and a line of light. |
| 30–45 s | 64–96 | **drop A** | **1982 Schubert polynomials** (hero), 1984, 1990, 1992, **1993 RC-graphs** (hero), 1994. |
| 45–52.5 s | 96–112 | break (half-time) | 1997, 1999, 2002, then **c^ν_λμ = #?**: the count cycles TABLEAUX, HONEYCOMBS… and freezes on a red "?"; a triangle of light draws itself in the silent beat. |
| 52.5–67.5 s | 112–144 | **drop B** | The triangle becomes **2003 puzzles** (hero); 2005 – 2018. |
| 67.5–70.8 s | 144–151 | run | 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, faster and faster. |

The 32-second cut shows the same results except 1990, 1994 and 2022.

Every citation was checked against the publisher or a bibliographic database.
Every visual is a computation checked in Python, for example:

- the five reduced pipe dreams of 1432, matching 𝔖₁₄₃₂ from divided differences
- the two Knutson–Tao puzzles for c^{321}_{21,21} = 2 in Gr(3,6)
- c^{432}_{21,321} = 2 by its two Littlewood–Richardson tableaux
- w = 42531 contains 4231 (singular), v = 43521 avoids 3412 and 4231 (smooth)
- the Fomin–Stanley product expanding into the Schubert polynomials of S₃
- 𝔖₁₃₄₂ · 𝔖₃₂₁ = 𝔖₃₄₂₁ + 𝔖₄₂₃₁ + 𝔖₄₃₁₂ (separated descents at k = 2)

## Rendering

```
python3 score75.py                                    # out/music75.wav + beats.js
node render.mjs --html film.html --workers 3 --blur 4 --preset slow --crf 12 --grain 0 \
  --audio out/music75.wav --out out/master75.mp4
./deliver.sh out/master75.mp4 schubert-history-75s.mp4 out/schubert-history-75s-send.mp4
```

`render.mjs` renders 4 sub-frames per frame across a 180° shutter that opens at
the frame time. Every cut snaps to a frame boundary, so no frame mixes two shots.
`deliver.sh` adds film grain to the repository copy and makes a grain-free copy
under 30 MiB for sending.

The 32-second cut: `python3 score.py`, then `node render.mjs --html film32.html
--to 32 ... --audio out/music32.wav`. `resync.py` can re-time that cut to
another recording (for example `python3 resync.py song.mp3 --start 95 --dur 32`
writes `beats32.js`).

## Files

- `film.html`, `film.js`, `content.js`, `beats.js`: the 75-second cut. Every frame is a pure function of time.
- `film32.html`, `film32.js`, `content32.js`, `beats32.js`: the 32-second cut.
- `motifs/*.js`: one visual per result. `motif-test.html?id=<motif>&dur=1.875&stage=1` previews one on the centre stage.
- `engine.js`: shared drawing helpers.
- `render.mjs`: deterministic frame-by-frame rendering with headless Chromium, motion blur and ffmpeg encoding.
- `score75.py`, `score.py`: the original scores and their beat maps.
- `deliver.sh`: the final encodes. `contact.sh`: contact sheets of stills. `resync.py`: re-times the 32-second cut.
